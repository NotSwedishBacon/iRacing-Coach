# Springs

### Control car balance with springs

Springs or spring rate if you prefer, have a dramatic effect on the balance of your setup because general the end of the car with the softer springs will have more grip than the end of the car with the harder springs.

#### Front Springs

As you stiffen the front springs you will reduce oversteer and the car will feel like it responds more directly or more quickly to your steering inputs on initial turn in. Likewise, softening the front springs will increase oversteer of the car but it will feel slightly more lazy on initial turn in.

#### Rear Springs

Stiffer rear springs will increase oversteer, and softer rear springs will reduce oversteer. But as mentioned if you go too soft in the rear in combination with a rear ride heigh that is very low you might lose aero downforce in the rear which for high speed corners like the kink at Road America you could increase oversteer.

### Reduce Drag for high speed tracks

For high speed tracks like Daytona or Lemans having lower springs allows the aero downforce to push the car closer to the ground which will create less drag. But if you go too low to the ground you will lose downforce because there is an optimal ride height to create maximum downforce. So sometimes a stiffer spring with a lower ride height is the best option because the stiffer spring does a better job of reducing the ride height variance and thus keeps the car more in the optimal aero window. So as always it is about finding the optimal spring rate for the track and balance you are looking for.

### Debunking myths about springs and weight transfer

Spring rates do affect weight transfer but not in the way most think it does. This is important to understand so that you can make the proper decisions about having overall stiff or overall soft spring rates on your setup.

#### Myth - Softer springs all around allow more weight transfer

The amount of weight transfer front to rear or side to side on a car is the same if you had 1000 lbs springs at all four corners or 500 lb springs at all for corners. This may be counter intuitive, but it is true. Many will think the reason why increasing the front spring rate causes less oversteer, is because the car transfers less weight forward under trail braking. This is simply not the case. You are likely skeptical as I was when I first learned this but read on.

#### Truth - The difference in spring rates front to rear cause a difference in lateral weight transfer at each axle, which in turn affects car balance

A car having 1000 units(lbs) springs all around will have the same lateral and longitudinal weight transfer as a car having 500 units(lbs) springs all around.  However, that has softer springs up front and harder springs in the rear will cause more lateral weight transfer at the rear and less lateral weight transfer at the front making the car oversteer more.  So, it is the difference in spring rates front to rear that causes this balance change.

**The same spring rate front and rear have the same lateral weight transfer at each axle**

Let's use some numbers to help illustrate the point. We will start by assuming the car has an equal static weight distribution front to rear and equal spring rates front and rear as follows:

* Front Spring Rate: 1000 units
* Rear Spring Rate: 1000 units

If we assume the car weighs 2000 units, that will mean each corner will have the same 500 units of vertical load on the tires.

* FL: 500 units FR: 500 units
* RL: 500 units RL: 500 units

Now let's assume we are going through a right hand corner and this causes 250 units weight transfer from the inside tires to the outside tires increasing the difference between the vertical load of the two front tires to be 500 units, and the same 500 units for the rear, totaling 1000 units of of total weight transfer. This will make the vertical load on the tires look like this, which would not change if the spring rates were softer (for example 500 units springs).

* FL: 750 units FL: 250 units
* RL: 750 units RL: 250 units

**Different front and rear spring rates, create different amounts of lateral weight transfer at each axle**

If we were to reduce the spring rate from 1000 units to 500 units at the front but kept the rears at 1000 units what happens to the weight transfer? So here are the new spring rates.

* Front Spring Rate: 500 units
* Rear Spring Rate: <mark style="color:red;">1000</mark> units

The rear of the car will roll less and the front will roll more, and under braking the front will dive more, making the front CG (center of gravity) lower, effectively adding more rake, which in of itself will give the car more steering (see [ride height ](/learn-car-setup-for-iracing/setup-changes/ride-height.md)section). 

When the car starts to turn this causes the rear inside tire vertical load to reduce compared to before the spring rate change. You can think of it like the rear inside tire is now lifted off the ground more assuming a stiff chassis, even though in most cases you are not actually lifting the tire off the ground. But it helps illustrate the point that the inside rear tire has less vertical load. The outside rear tire now has more vertical load trtransferredrom the rear inside tire, causing a greater difference in vertical load between the rear tires, which reduces lateral grip at the rear of the car.

In contrast the front tires now have a more even vertical load difference between them adding more grip to the front tires, in addition to the additional grip given to the front from the change in rake.  All of this adds up to more grip at the front and less grip at the rear.

So now the vertical load distribution will look like this. Note that the total lateral weight distribution is still the same 1000 units, but now most of that has gone from the rear inside tire to the rear outside tire. In this example the front tire lateral weight transfer was decreased from 500 units to 300 units and rear tire lateral weight transfer was increased from 500 units to 700 units.  As mentioned above, the more weight transfer across an axel the less grip that axel will have.

* FL: <mark style="color:red;">650</mark> units FL: <mark style="color:red;">350</mark> units
* RL: <mark style="color:red;">850</mark> units RL: <mark style="color:red;">150</mark> units

### Stiff suspension benefits

* Reduces dynamic changes to toe because of reduced suspension travel
* The car responds more quickly and consistently to your inputs
* Allows you to reduce ride height for less drag but do a better job at keeping you in the aero downforce window
* Allows your car to stay closer to the optimal ride height for optimal aero downforce

### Stiff suspension downsides

* Tires don't stay in touch with the track surface as easily when you encounter bumps which can reduce grip
* Car can be unsettled more by curb strikes causing unpredictable car balance
* Tracks that have big changes in the banking in a short distance (think the Dipper or Forest Elbow at Bathurst)  can unsettle the car balance more than more compliant softer springs.

<mark style="color:purple;">**Key take away**</mark>

<mark style="color:purple;">Use the difference in spring rates between the front and rear to affect the balance of the car.</mark>

<mark style="color:purple;">In general a stiffer all around spring setup is best except for really bumpy tracks, or if you need to do a lot of curb strikes.</mark>

For example, Monza would likely benefit from softer springs because of there are some significant curb strikes to absorb. Also, softer springs can reduce drag as the car is lower. However, be careful as if you go too low in the rear due to soft springs and low ride heights you could lose too much rear end grip in the medium-high speed last corner. Sebring needs softer springs clearly because of the big bumps mainly in the last corner sunset.

### Handling Scenarios

In each of the following scenarios, here are things you will help you improve the handling by making changes to front and rear spring rates. You don't need to do all of the suggested changes listed below in these scenarios. It is best to start at the top of the list and do one at a time.

#### Loose Off Power

* Increase front spring rate
* Decrease rear spring rate

#### Loose On Power

* Decrease rear spring rate
* Increase front spring rate

#### Push Off Power

* Decrease front spring rate
* Increase rear spring rate

#### Push On Power

* Increase rear spring rate
* Decrease front spring rate
